Right now

  • Aug 2026 to present

    Communications Associate

    Florida State University, Departments of Geography and Demography · Tallahassee

    I'm the communications person for two academic departments at once, about 25 hours a week. Geography and Demography each have their own faculty, events and expectations, so a big part of the job is keeping both on track and knowing which standard applies where.

    • Databases. The departmental records were inconsistent and hard to search, so I restructured them on my own initiative. Now anyone in the office can find what they need without asking me first.
    • Websites. I make ongoing WordPress edits and content updates for both department sites inside FSU's brand template, and I'm on the team revamping the Geography site. More Social Sciences sites are planned.
    • Content. I write student and research spotlight articles, design event graphics, run the departments' social media and write the communications reports for both.

    What I got out of it. This is the business side of IT, and I took it on purpose because it was outside my technical lane. It made me a better writer and a more careful editor, and it taught me to work for two sets of expectations without dropping either. An employer gets someone who can clean up a messy data set, write for a general audience and ship finished work on a deadline.

    WordPress · Data organization · Writing · Event graphics · Social media

  • Fall 2026 to present

    AI Team Lead, Matchob

    FSU AWS Cloud Club · team project

    Matchob is a Chrome extension from FSU's AWS Cloud Club that tailors a resume to a job posting. I lead the AI team, the part that matches each requirement in the posting to the resume and checks that every suggestion stays true to it.

    • Planning. Each sprint I decide what the AI team builds, break it into tasks a teammate can pick up and review every pull request before it merges.
    • Guardrails. I built the checks that compare every suggestion with the original resume, so the extension can't claim a skill or a job the person doesn't have. That's 36 attack tests and 120 automated tests on the AI code so far.
    • Stack. The AI side runs on Python and Amazon Bedrock. The extension is TypeScript, so I work on both sides of that line.

    What I got out of it. This is the first time I've been responsible for other people's code and not just my own. I've learned to write a task clearly enough that a teammate can run with it, to give review feedback that is specific without being harsh and to plan a sprint around what the team can actually finish. Those are the habits any dev or support team needs from a new hire on day one.

    Python · Amazon Bedrock · TypeScript · Sprint planning · Code review

  • Jul 2026 to present

    Nursery Manager

    Dickerson Landscaping · Tallahassee

    Dickerson Landscaping keeps a private nursery for the plants its crews install. I run it, mostly on my own.

    • Inventory. I track what's on hand and keep the standing stock healthy, so material is ready the day a crew needs it and a job doesn't stall because a plant isn't there or isn't fit to go in the ground.
    • Planning. I plan my own week around watering, weather and what's going out, and I catch problems before they reach a job site.

    What I got out of it. This is the clearest example on my resume of owning an operation end to end. Nobody checks my work hour by hour; the condition of the nursery is the check. That's the same accountability an IT team needs from whoever is on the ticket queue or watching the network: notice problems first, fix what you can and report the rest clearly.

    Inventory · Operations · Ownership · Planning

Before

  • May 2026

    FSUshadow, Information Security Engineering

    Florida Blue · Jacksonville

    • Selected through FSU's competitive matched job shadow program for a full day with Florida Blue's enterprise Information Security Engineering team.
    • I watched Palo Alto Networks technology in production: live troubleshooting, security hardening and enterprise policy enforcement.

    What I got out of it. One day isn't hands-on experience with the tools, and I don't list it that way. What it gave me is a picture of how an enterprise security team actually spends its day, and what troubleshooting and hardening look like when the network is live and the policies are real. It connected my Wireshark training and LanSentinel to real work, and it's a big part of why information security is on my list for 2027.

    Palo Alto Networks · Security hardening · Policy enforcement
